# ENTRY — Competitor Note — Guardian X — 2026-09-02

Competitor intelligence captured from operator screenshots and public Guardian X web presence observed on 2026-09-02.

> **Evidence discipline:** public pricing/features can change. Treat this note as a dated market benchmark, not a permanent price sheet. Claims below distinguish what was directly visible in operator screenshots from broader interpretation.

## Identity / market position

- Brand: **Guardian X**.
- Public site observed: `guardianxhn.com`.
- The product presents itself as a residential/community operations platform and overlaps directly with ENTRY in visitor/access management.
- Public positioning extends beyond access into broader residential administration/security operations.

## Directly observed public features

Operator screenshots showed Guardian X publicly presenting:

- **Multi-Residencial** — described as ideal for administrative companies managing multiple residential communities from one centralized account.
- **Reporte de Incidencias** — detailed event/incident records with historical log for audit and decision-making.
- A security-operations feature area referencing control of **turnos, consignas y rondas** so security staff can follow daily protocols.

This makes Guardian X relevant not only as an ENTRY access competitor, but also as evidence that the market may support a higher-level buyer managing several communities.

## Immediate pricing implication for ENTRY

Guardian X creates a useful local benchmark around the 50–100-home segment.

ENTRY's current internal pricing has a large jump between:

- up to 50 homes: L 2,500/month;
- 51–200 homes: L 5,000/month.

A community in roughly the 51–100-home range could therefore compare ENTRY's current L 5,000 internal tier against Guardian X's publicly advertised L 3,975 tier for up to 100 homes.

This does **not** authorize a price change. It creates a research question:

> Does ENTRY need a smoother pricing transition in the 50–100-home band once unit economics, support burden and competitive positioning are understood?

Andalucía context:

- Guardian X's visible up-to-100-home public list price annualizes to **L 47,700/year**.
- ENTRY's opportunity-specific verbal commitment for Residencial Andalucía is **L 35,000/year**.
- This comparison suggests the Andalucía figure is below Guardian X's visible public benchmark, but profitability for Minerva still depends on ENTRY Unit Economics v1.

## Strategic signal — Multi-Residencial

The most important non-price signal is Guardian X's explicit **Multi-Residencial** positioning:

> "Ideal para empresas administradoras. Gestiona múltiples residenciales desde una sola cuenta centralizada."

This independently supports the existing ENTRY Developer/Admin Channel hypothesis: there may be a commercially meaningful buyer above the individual patronato level.

Potential buyer types to investigate:

- residential administration companies;
- developers/operators managing several projects;
- security/operations groups with multiple communities;
- portfolio owners or project groups that want one control plane across several residentials.

This does **not** prove that developers are Guardian X's primary customers. It does prove that at least one local competitor considers multi-community administration valuable enough to market publicly.

## Competitive posture for ENTRY

Do not respond by copying Guardian X feature-for-feature.

Current discipline remains:

- ENTRY's clearest wedge is visitor/access authorization, validation, recording and operational clarity;
- FIRST DOOR remains active;
- broader modules should be driven by repeated buyer evidence, not competitor anxiety;
- multi-residential capability should be researched before product scope is expanded.

Potential positioning distinction to preserve:

> Guardian X appears to market a broad residential-management suite. ENTRY can continue to lead with a highly focused, complete visitor/access operation and expand only where field evidence supports it.

## Near-term research pending — Multi-Residential / Admin Channel

**Status:** `Research pending — near term`.

Purpose:

> Determine whether multi-residential administration is a real second buyer/channel for ENTRY, what organizations in Honduras perform that role, and whether the opportunity is large enough to justify product/commercial planning.

Research questions:

1. Which companies in SPS/Honduras administer multiple residential communities?
2. Are these companies independent administrators, developers that retain management, security providers, property-management firms, or mixed models?
3. Who owns the access/visitor-system purchasing decision in those arrangements?
4. Do they want one centralized dashboard/account across communities?
5. Do they require separate branding per community, or is one platform acceptable?
6. How do they price their own management service to patronatos/developers?
7. How many communities can a typical administrator manage?
8. What information/actions must be centralized vs isolated per community?
9. What happens to software contracts when a developer hands a project to a patronato?
10. Which competitors besides Guardian X offer multi-community administration locally?
11. What would ENTRY need technically for a true portfolio/admin layer, and what already exists in Minerva Console/ENTRY architecture?
12. Would one administrator relationship materially reduce customer-acquisition cost compared with one-patronato-at-a-time selling?

Suggested output when research is executed:

- market map of named multi-residential administrators/developers in SPS/Honduras;
- 5–10 target organizations with evidence and contact routes;
- competitor matrix for multi-residential capabilities/pricing;
- recommended discovery script;
- technical gap assessment for ENTRY;
- recommendation: `ignore / monitor / pilot / formalize channel`.

## Decision rule

Do **not** build a multi-residential module merely because Guardian X has one.

Promote this from research to product/commercial scope only if we find repeated evidence that:

1. identifiable organizations actually manage multiple communities;
2. they have a visitor/access problem ENTRY can solve;
3. they can influence or control vendor selection;
4. one relationship can realistically produce multiple deployments;
5. economics justify any additional centralized-management complexity.
