# ENTRY — Developer / Operator / Admin Channel Research — 2026-09-03

Web research executed to identify Honduran organizations that may control residential-project operations, amenities, access/security technology, or multi-community administration.

> **Status:** Controlled commercial experiment approved. FIRST DOOR remains active and unchanged. This research does not authorize new product features, pricing tiers, white-label apps, or a multi-residential dashboard.

## Strategic conclusion

The working channel should no longer be described only as a `Developer Channel`.

A better current label is:

> **Developer / Operator / Admin Channel**

Reason: the organization that physically builds a residential project is not necessarily the party that chooses or operates visitor/access software. Relevant decision actors can include developers, project operators, condominium administrators, property-management firms, security operators, or commercial organizations that retain operational control.

The economic motivation for testing this channel is meaningful:

- patronato prospecting has high discovery/friction cost;
- public developers/admin firms expose websites, email, phone, project portfolios and active sales pipelines;
- one relationship may potentially create several deployments;
- competitor evidence from Guardian X and Tu Pase independently supports multi-community/admin buyers as a real market pattern worth validating.

## Controlled experiment — approved

Do **not** mass-email the market.

Initial experiment:

1. Map and preserve 8–10 credible organizations.
2. Select three distinct buyer/channel archetypes.
3. Contact only 2–3 initially with highly personalized outreach.
4. Objective of first contact is a 20-minute discovery conversation, not an immediate software sale.
5. Learn who chooses access technology, when it is chosen, who pays, whether it spans multiple projects, and what happens at patronato handoff.
6. After the first 2–3 conversations, decide whether to continue, pause or deepen the channel.

Recommended first three experiments:

1. **CONCASA** — multi-project developer test.
2. **DC Group** — active/new-project developer test.
3. **BackOffice Outsourcing & Consulting (BOOC)** — multi-community administration/operator test.

## First outreach posture

Do not open with a feature list or price.

Recommended conversation frame:

> "Estamos desarrollando ENTRY como plataforma de gestión de visitantes y accesos para comunidades residenciales. Hemos visto que el control de acceso digital ya forma parte de la propuesta de valor de proyectos nuevos, y queremos entender cómo ustedes manejan esa decisión desde la etapa de desarrollo hasta la entrega al patronato."

Core questions:

- ¿Quién decide el sistema de visitantes/acceso de un proyecto nuevo?
- ¿En qué etapa se selecciona?
- ¿Forma parte de las amenidades que ayudan a vender el proyecto?
- ¿Quién paga y administra el sistema durante la etapa de ventas/desarrollo?
- ¿Qué ocurre cuando se forma o toma control el patronato?
- ¿Un mismo proveedor puede cubrir varios proyectos?
- ¿Se requiere una marca/app distinta por proyecto o aceptan una plataforma común con identidad por comunidad?
- Para administradores: ¿cuántas comunidades manejan y qué información necesitan centralizar?

## Product discipline

No product commitment comes from this research alone.

Current rules:

- ENTRY remains the product/brand by default;
- community-specific identity/configuration can live inside ENTRY;
- no separate native app per residential by default;
- no white-label promise unless future enterprise economics justify it;
- no multi-residential dashboard until buyer evidence defines the actual workflows;
- no developer-specific pricing yet;
- competitor features are benchmarks, not roadmap commands.
