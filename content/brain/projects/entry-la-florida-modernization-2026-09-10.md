# ENTRY — Residencial La Florida modernization strategy

**Date:** 2026-09-10
**Status:** Idea captured for later evaluation
**Type:** Commercial strategy / field hypothesis
**Product:** ENTRY
**Target:** Residencial La Florida

## Context

Rudy lives in Residencial La Florida and has identified an operational weakness in the current access setup: when electrical power is lost, the local Wi-Fi/network equipment can shut down, which can leave the access system without connectivity.

This is a direct field observation from Rudy and should be treated as operator-provided evidence. It does **not** by itself prove that the ISP remains available during an outage or that every access component can be kept operational with a small UPS.

The current monthly price paid by La Florida for its existing access system is **unknown**. Rudy informally estimates it could be around HNL 5,000/month, but this is a hypothesis only and must **not** be treated as a confirmed price or used as official competitive evidence.

## Commercial idea

Return to Residencial La Florida with a modernization proposal built around three linked benefits:

1. **Modernize the access operation with ENTRY.**
2. **Offer an economically more beneficial proposal than the community's current service, but only after receiving evidence of what they actually pay.**
3. **Improve local continuity during power outages by adding UPS backup for the essential low-power connectivity equipment at the gate.**

The commercial positioning should not be "send us your bill and we will undercut it." The intended message is that Minerva wants to make modernization financially attractive for the community.

Suggested framing:

> As an incentive to facilitate modernization of Residencial La Florida's access system, Minerva Technologies can use the current service cost as a reference. With a bill, statement, or other evidence of the amount the community currently pays, Minerva will prepare a more beneficial economic proposal, seeking to reduce the community's cost without sacrificing ENTRY capabilities, support, and included improvements.

Short verbal positioning:

> The idea is that modernizing the system should not mean paying more; Minerva wants to provide an economic incentive for the community to make the change.

Use language such as **"more beneficial proposal"** rather than guaranteeing **"always cheaper"** until Minerva understands what the current contract actually includes (software, hardware, maintenance, cameras, physical gate equipment, etc.).

## UPS continuity concept

Candidate observed on 2026-09-10:

- Member's Selection ST-1500MS
- 1500 VA / 900 W
- Observed retail price: approximately HNL 3,999.95
- Status: candidate only; not purchased, approved, or finalized

The UPS should be positioned as part of **continuity of the local access/connectivity infrastructure**, not merely as an accessory.

Before committing to a specific unit or runtime, Minerva should inspect the gate and identify the actual devices that must remain powered, such as:

- ISP modem / ONT
- router
- network switch or access point
- low-power access controller, if applicable

Do not promise a specific number of backup hours until tested against the real load.

Important technical boundary:

> A UPS can keep protected local equipment powered during an electrical outage, but it does not guarantee Internet availability if the ISP's upstream infrastructure also fails.

Do not assume the selected UPS can power the physical gate motor, barrier motor, or other high-load equipment. Those require separate electrical assessment.

## Potential future evolution

A stronger continuity package could later combine:

- UPS backup for local network/access equipment; and
- secondary 4G/5G Internet failover if the primary ISP connection fails.

This may eventually become an ENTRY continuity offering, but it is **not part of the current committed scope** and should not be sold before technical and economic evaluation.

## Proposal structure for later evaluation

A future La Florida proposal can be intentionally short and patronato-oriented:

- objective: modernize access operations;
- ENTRY capabilities and support;
- identified continuity opportunity during power outages;
- UPS-backed local connectivity concept;
- economic modernization incentive based on verified current spend;
- final economic offer prepared only after Minerva reviews evidence of the current service cost;
- implementation/onboarding plan;
- clear technical caveat that local power continuity does not guarantee upstream ISP continuity.

The first proposal should generally avoid publishing a speculative ENTRY price before the current service benchmark is verified. Once La Florida provides evidence of its current cost, Minerva can model the offer using real ENTRY Unit Economics and choose a price that is both attractive to the community and sustainable for Minerva.

## Validation required before action

Before turning this idea into a formal quote:

- verify the current service price with a bill, statement, contract, or equivalent evidence;
- understand what that current price includes;
- inspect the gate's network/access equipment and power draw;
- determine whether the ISP itself stays online during local outages;
- choose and test the appropriate UPS;
- run the proposed commercial price through Seshat Unit Economics;
