# ENTRY — Field Report — 2026-08-22

Field-discovery notes from the operator's 2026-08-22 residential scouting round in the Zapotal / Bermejo area of San Pedro Sula. This report is commercial knowledge only; no ENTRY runtime or product scope is changed by these observations.

> Evidence discipline: home counts, organizational structure, prior-vendor activity, patronato references, and system details below are **operator-provided from guard/security conversations unless explicitly described as direct observation**. Treat guard statements as useful field evidence, not independently verified administration facts.

## Mission objective

Continue FIRST DOOR discovery by identifying communities with:

- controlled entrances and active security;
- manual visitor authorization through calls / paper;
- no stable digital visitor-management system;
- enough occupied homes to justify the workflow;
- a reachable patronato or administration;
- a credible next step such as a contact, delivery route, or agreed package handoff.

The round also tested a second lesson: a development can look attractive from the road but be a poor FIRST DOOR target if it is still developer-controlled or already has an installed access platform.

## Residencial Marsella — investigated / low FIRST DOOR priority

Operator reached Residencial Marsella and spoke with security.

Guard/security stated:

- approximately **11 families currently living there**;
- there is security and a controlled entrance;
- a digital access system/app is already in use;
- the system was described only as a proprietary/internal solution with an **"M"** visible/associated with it; exact product/vendor remains unknown;
- there is **no patronato yet**;
- when asked who handles the system, the guard referred to **"los de ventas"**;
- neighboring Residencial Provenza reportedly uses the same system.

Operator interpretation:

> Marsella appears to remain in a developer / sales-led phase rather than a mature patronato-led operating phase.

Do **not** treat the app as temporary or permanent without confirmation. The guard did not know what happens to the system once a patronato is formed.

Current classification:

> **Investigated — low FIRST DOOR priority for now.** Existing system + very low current occupancy + no patronato.

Strategic learning:

> New developments may enter operation with a developer-provided access system before residents organize a patronato. For this segment, the buyer may be the developer / sales organization rather than the future resident patronato.

## Colonia Los Robles — fragmented structure; one promising independent sector

Operator observed and investigated Los Robles as a colony divided into multiple passages / sectors.

Observed / reported:

- approximately **five passages/access points** were noticed during the round;
- individual passages have their own barriers / guard positions;
- at least some passages appear to have separate local administration;
- guards suggested there may also be a broader/general patronato, but the first two guards could not explain the structure or provide a useful contact;
- one guard described residents as difficult / highly particular, which is only that guard's opinion and must not be generalized as fact.

Commercial conclusion:

> **Do not treat Los Robles as one homogeneous prospect.** Individual passages/sectors may make security/access decisions independently.

Current classification:

> **Qualified → package preparation / agreed handoff path.**

Important scope note:

> The package should be addressed to the specific independent Los Robles sector/patronato, not to all of Colonia Los Robles, because decision-making appears fragmented.

Next objective:

1. Deliver the personalized FIRST DOOR package on 2026-08-23.

## Commercial learning from this round

### 1. A colony name may hide multiple independent buyers

Los Robles demonstrated that one geographic colony can contain multiple access-control units with different guards, barriers, patronatos, and decision paths.

> Qualification should follow the **actual operating/decision unit**, not just the map label.

### 2. One unhelpful guard does not necessarily close the location

The first Los Robles contacts produced almost no actionable information. A later independent security post produced a qualified lead and promised patronato delivery path.

This is **not** permission to pressure guards repeatedly. The useful principle is:

> In fragmented communities, one additional access point may represent a different organization rather than simply another employee of the same buyer.

### 3. New construction / sales-stage communities can already be digitized

Marsella and Provenza suggest a developer-channel model in which access technology can be installed before a resident patronato exists.

That segment may eventually require a separate go-to-market motion toward developers rather than the current FIRST DOOR patronato package.

### 4. Existing app = learning target, not first-wave replacement target

Alondra reinforces the existing attack order: communities with stable digital systems are useful competitive intelligence but are lower-friction targets only after ENTRY has a strong reference case.

### 5. Do not convert vague guard comments into buyer objections

Bermejo's uncertain "caro" recollection is exactly the kind of signal that must remain labeled as ambiguous until a patronato member explains what was quoted and what scope was included.
