# ENTRY — Developer Channel Hypothesis — 2026-08-23

Working commercial hypothesis created from recent ENTRY field discovery plus operator-observed residential marketing content in San Pedro Sula.

> **Status:** exploratory hypothesis only. This does **not** replace FIRST DOOR, does not create a new official pricing tier, and does not authorize product/engineering scope changes.

## Why this hypothesis exists

Recent field discovery showed that some newer residential developments can already have digital access technology before a resident patronato exists.

Examples already observed in the field:

- **Marsella:** security reported an existing digital access system/app, no patronato yet, and indicated that "los de ventas" manage the system.
- **Provenza:** reportedly follows a similar developer/sales-stage system pattern.

On 2026-08-23, the operator also surfaced two residential-marketing examples through Instagram:

- **Jardines del Este, SPS:** operator observed a residential marketing video that appeared to show a controlled entrance / barrier. Exact access workflow and vendor remain unverified.
- **Residencial Paraíso, SPS:** operator observed a marketing video explicitly presenting a gatehouse / controlled-access concept and QR access. Treat the QR claim as operator-observed marketing evidence until independently confirmed.

These examples suggest a potentially important market pattern:

> For new residential projects, digital access may function not only as an operational tool but also as a **marketed amenity** that helps communicate security, organization and modernity to prospective buyers.

## Core hypothesis

ENTRY may eventually support a second commercial motion in parallel with patronato-led FIRST DOOR:

### Motion A — FIRST DOOR / existing communities

Buyer:

- patronato;
- administration;
- established community decision structure.

Problem:

- calls;
- WhatsApp;
- paper logs;
- fragmented visitor authorization and access records.

Value proposition:

> Move an existing manual visitor/access operation into one digital workflow.

### Motion B — Developer Channel / new projects

Potential buyer or channel owner:

- residential developer;
- project administrator/operator;
- organization defining amenities and operational handoff;
- commercial/sales organization when it controls project operations;
- possibly a construction company only when it actually participates in system/amenity decisions.

The construction company should **not** automatically be assumed to be the buyer. In many cases it may execute the physical project while another entity decides technology, security and resident-facing amenities.

Potential value proposition:

> **Deliver the residential community with digital visitor and access management from day one.**

In this motion, ENTRY would not primarily be asking an established community to change an existing habit. It could instead become part of the project's original operating package and marketed amenity set.

## Why the developer channel could matter

### 1. Access technology may help sell the project

A buyer of a new home may interpret language such as QR access, controlled access, gatehouse, digital authorization or smart security as evidence that the development is modern and organized.

That means ENTRY's value may extend beyond gate operations into the developer's sales story.

### 2. One relationship can span multiple communities

A successful relationship with a developer or project operator could potentially cover several residential projects over time.

This is strategically different from winning one patronato at a time.

Potential future positioning:

> "ENTRY can serve as the visitor and access-management standard across your residential projects."

This is only a hypothesis until repeated developer/project evidence supports it.

### 3. Developer prospects leave a stronger public footprint

Compared with patronatos, developers and real-estate project organizations often expose more public information:

- project names;
- developer/brand names;
- sales contacts;
- social media;
- amenities;
- construction stage;
- additional projects in the same portfolio.

This can materially reduce prospect-research cost and allow a more informed first conversation.

## Critical qualification: identify the real decision-maker

Do not equate "inmobiliaria" with buyer.

A real-estate agency may only market/sell inventory for somebody else.

For every project, determine who actually decides:

- access/security technology;
- operational systems;
- amenity package;
- vendor selection;
- who pays before community handoff;
- what happens after a resident patronato forms.

Possible decision actors include developer, administrator, project owner, sales organization, security contractor or another operating entity.

## Strategic handoff question

One of the most important questions in this hypothesis is:

> **What happens to the access platform when the developer finishes selling/operating the project and the community transitions to a patronato?**

Research should determine whether the system:

- remains as a permanent community service;
- is transferred to the patronato;
- is developer-paid only during sales;
- requires a new contract after handoff;
- is replaced;
- or is bundled with another security/automation provider.

This handoff point may become a major ENTRY differentiation opportunity if the product can provide continuity from developer launch into patronato operation.

## Brand / app policy hypothesis

The preferred default remains:

> **ENTRY is the product. Each residential community operates inside ENTRY with its own identity/configuration.**

Do **not** default to creating a separate app for every residential project under that residential's own name.

Avoid an operating model that becomes:

- "Residencial Paraíso App";
- "Marsella App";
- "Provenza App";
- etc.

That approach can create unnecessary complexity in releases, app-store management, support, QA and long-term maintenance.

Preferred developer-facing language could eventually be:

> "The residential project includes digital visitor and access management through ENTRY."

### White-label exception

A large developer may eventually request its own brand / white-label experience.

Do not reject the idea categorically, but do **not** include it in standard residential pricing or promise it during discovery.

Treat white-label as a possible **enterprise/commercial exception** whose economics, product architecture, support burden and contract value must justify the added complexity.

## Research mode — do not sell this motion prematurely

FIRST DOOR remains the active sales motion.

The Developer Channel should currently operate as a structured learning track.

Target before deciding whether to formalize the motion:

> Collect approximately **10–15 useful observations/projects** and look for repeated developers, operating models, access vendors and handoff patterns.

For each new residential project surfaced through Instagram, field driving, ads or referrals, capture:

1. Project/residential name.
2. Location.
3. Development/construction stage.
4. Developer/project owner if identifiable.
5. Organization commercializing the project.
6. Who appears to administer it before patronato formation.
7. Security/access amenities advertised.
8. Whether QR/app/digital access is explicitly advertised.
9. Observed or reported vendor/platform if known.
10. Whether the same developer/operator has other projects.
11. Who appears to pay for the system.
12. What happens to the system after patronato handoff.
13. Best available contact/decision route.
14. Evidence source and confidence level.

Important evidence rule:

> Social-media marketing is a **radar**, not technical confirmation. Record what was advertised/observed, then independently qualify the real workflow and decision structure.

## Discovery conversation for developer/operator contacts

The first conversation should be research-oriented, not a hard pitch.

Suggested frame:

> "Estamos desarrollando ENTRY como plataforma de gestión de visitantes y accesos para comunidades residenciales. Hemos visto que el control de acceso ya forma parte de la propuesta de valor de proyectos nuevos, y queremos entender cómo ustedes resuelven esa parte desde desarrollo hasta entrega al patronato."

Questions to learn from:

- ¿Quién decide el sistema de acceso de un proyecto nuevo?
- ¿En qué etapa lo seleccionan?
- ¿Lo consideran parte de las amenidades que ayudan a vender el proyecto?
- ¿Quién paga y administra el servicio mientras el proyecto está en etapa de ventas?
- ¿Qué pasa con ese servicio cuando se forma el patronato?
- ¿Prefieren una plataforma reconocible del proveedor o suelen exigir marca propia por proyecto?
- ¿Un mismo proveedor puede cubrir varios proyectos del portafolio?

## Commercial discipline

Until evidence says otherwise:

- **FIRST DOOR does not change.**
- Do not slow Los Robles, Bermejo, Andalucía, El Carmen or El Limonar to chase this new idea.
- Do not build developer-specific features yet.
- Do not promise white-label apps.
- Do not create a developer pricing table yet.
- Do not assume every QR-controlled new development is an ENTRY prospect; many may already be locked into another provider.
- Use repeated observations to identify where ENTRY could enter earlier in the project lifecycle.

## Working strategic framing

Current commercial map can now be thought of as two distinct hypotheses:

> **Existing communities:** ENTRY as digitalization of a manual access operation.
>
> **New developments:** ENTRY as an access-management amenity and operating layer included from launch.

The first is already being executed through FIRST DOOR.

The second is now approved for **exploration and evidence collection only**.
